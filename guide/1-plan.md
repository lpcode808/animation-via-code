# 1. Plan the piece

Decide what the video teaches, in what order and how long each idea holds, before any code exists. Most bad videos were bad plans drawn well.

Lessons are tagged **confirmed** (held up across many pieces), **tentative** (seen once or twice) or **contested** (sources disagree; see [where the notes disagree](appendix.md#where-the-notes-disagree)).

## Lessons

**Plan before you draw.** (confirmed) Spend the first couple of minutes on a beat list: what's on screen, what the caption says, and how long it holds. Agents that start drawing immediately produce pretty scenes in the wrong order.

**Short prompts work if they cover five things.** (confirmed) Length and purpose, audience, one or two taste constraints (more than three start to conflict), source material, and permission to go big ("go all out"). Without that last one, agents default to a safe, thin first draft. You don't need to specify tooling, frame rate, codec or palette.

**Write a "done means" line.** (confirmed) For example: "done means a new person watching can understand the concept." It gives both you and the agent a test for every review.

**Expect the agent to flag conflicts.** (confirmed) If the length and the content can't both be true (the song runs 45 s, you asked for 30), a good agent says so before coding and picks a stated default. That's not stalling.

**Storyboard at sketch fidelity first.** (confirmed) A rough shot list or pencil-style frames are cheap to fix. Ask for it, give notes, then say "ok, build it".

**Five to seven new ideas a minute.** (tentative) A first-time viewer absorbs about that many ideas in 60 seconds. Beats (on-screen moments) can come faster, because several beats can serve one idea. If the plan has nine ideas, ask which two to cut.

**Pick an order for the idea, not the topic.** (confirmed, for explainers) A beat order that worked again and again: an everyday **analogy**, then a **diagram**, then a worked **example**, then the idea in **plain English**, and only then the **technical** name or formula. Never put those labels on screen; they're for the planner.

**Show the skill, don't describe it.** (confirmed) "Watch the gear mesh" beats "gears transfer torque". If the caption could be read without the picture and lose nothing, the picture isn't working.

**Before and after.** (tentative) A quick "without this / with this" contrast holds attention better than a straight walkthrough, especially at the start.

**Compress the repetitive middle.** (tentative) When a process repeats (printing layers, sanding, a loop of code), show one cycle properly, then a short montage, then the result.

**End on something usable.** (confirmed) Close on a recap line, a question to try, or the one rule to remember, not on a logo or a fade.

**Use the source's own words and structure.** (tentative) Keep the source's verbs, turn the author's own metaphors into the visuals, and when a document's prose and its tables disagree, follow the tables. If a metaphor maps parts to parts, keep that mapping the same all the way through.

**Give each video its own identity.** (confirmed) After a run of pieces, the same plucks, pads, chimes and card layouts crept into everything. Choose the sound and look for this subject, on purpose.

**Time box.** (contested) One target was 15 minutes from prompt to MP4 for a 15 to 30 second clip: plan by minute 2, a contact sheet by minute 7, review by minute 10. See [where the notes disagree](appendix.md#where-the-notes-disagree) for how often that actually happened.

## Pitfalls

Bugs that each cost real time, with the fix.

**Can't download a reference image or asset.**
Agent containers often block image hosts, design tools and model hubs. Ask the human for an export instead of trying mirrors.
