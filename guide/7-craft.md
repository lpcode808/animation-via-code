# 7. Make it legible

Sizes, contrast and small honesty labels that decide whether the video works on a phone or at the back of a room.

Lessons are tagged **confirmed** (held up across many pieces), **tentative** (seen once or twice) or **contested** (sources disagree; see [where the notes disagree](appendix.md#where-the-notes-disagree)).

## Lessons

**Text sizes.** (confirmed) At 1080p: captions about 74 px, labels about 44 px. Anything under 24 px sits on a solid panel. Keep the bottom 15% free for players' controls and platform captions.

**Contrast.** (confirmed) At least 3:1 for lines and tracks against the background; coloured text needs a darker ink than the matching fill.

**Show viewpoint changes.** (confirmed) When switching from a flat diagram to a 3D or isometric view, show a small orientation card or animate the projection from flat to iso. Hard cuts between views lost reviewers every time.

**Cause before effect.** (confirmed) Draw the push before the motion, the input before the output.

**Label your cheats.** (confirmed) "Sped up", "not to scale", "lines drawn thicker than real". Name the simplification in a short label, and use one slow-motion rate per video, not several.

**Date anything current.** (confirmed) "As of March 2026" on any figure that can change.

**A plain stand-in character.** (tentative) For "you could do this" pieces, a nondescript character with second-person captions ("you sand the edge") worked better than a named person.

**Both ends as strengths.** (tentative) In pieces about personality or learning styles, show each end of a scale as a strength, never one as the deficit.

**Magnifier for detail.** (tentative) A close-up circle connected to its source by a cone reads better than cutting to a zoomed shot.

**Push transitions need care.** (confirmed) Sliding a whole scene sideways looks jittery unless offsets are whole pixels and a little motion blur is averaged in.

**Hand-drawn wobble at 4 fps.** (tentative) "Boiling" sketchy lines (redrawing them slightly differently) about four times a second reads as hand-drawn without being distracting.

**Matching a reference clip.** (tentative) When remaking a real video, solve each shot's camera from the reference and key motion to its exact frames. If the reference has burned-in captions, cover that band before adding your own type.

**Word-synced captions.** (tentative) For a recorded talk, page captions one sentence at a time, at most 3 lines, at least 0.6 s per page, with the current word highlighted.

**One graphic per sentence, one color per idea.** (tentative) In an edit of a talk, give each sentence at most one graphic (diagram, chip, timeline, mock-up), keep one color per idea everywhere, and use 2 or 3 fonts.

**Cut-outs switch in with a wipe.** (tentative) Run background removal only on the frames where text passes behind the speaker or they leave their frame, and switch into the cut-out with a wipe so mask flicker doesn't show.

## Pitfalls

Bugs that each cost real time, with the fix.

**Text runs out of its panel when a window shrinks.**
Clip each panel's text to the panel. One case was only visible in the final-MP4 contact sheet.

**Things move in steps instead of smoothly.**
Canvas text snaps to whole pixels. For slow text motion, move a pre-rendered image instead, or accept it and move faster.
